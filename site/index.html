The problem

Nobody knows whether the work happened.

Somewhere right now a technician is ticking a box that says brake pads replaced. Maybe they were. The record says so either way, because the record is a claim by an interested party stored in a system built to accept it.

Cost

Disputes you cannot win

Eight months later a customer disputes the service. You have a signature and a date. They have a lawyer. Nothing in your file establishes what actually took place.

Risk

Skipped work you never see

A machine in the workshop is a machine not earning. Falsified maintenance records are a recognised category of fraud in every industry that has bothered to look.

Waste

Good work you cannot prove

The technician who did the job properly is in exactly the same position as the one who did not. There is no way to be better and no reward for being better.

How it works

Aviation set the standard. Delivery worked out the price.

An aircraft logbook is legally binding and costs more than most machines are worth. A courier proves an unsupervised job in four seconds for pennies. Warrant is the first at the second's price.

STEP 01

Describe the job once

Say what the work is in plain language. An agent interviews you until nothing is left to interpret, then compiles a versioned procedure a machine can check.

STEP 02

Your technician works it

One step at a time on their phone, the way a courier is walked through a drop. Capture, next. Nothing is typed in and nothing waits on a spinner.

STEP 03

Instruments speak directly

Pair a torque wrench, a gauge, a caliper, a reader — or a sensor you built yourself. The number arrives with a tool identity and a timestamp, untouched by human hands.

STEP 04

Verified steps act

Stock decrements. A purchase order drafts itself. History updates. And the machine is not released until every step holds up.

What "verified" means

Three kinds of fact, never allowed to blur.

Warrant never claims a job was good. It closes off the ways the record could be false and states exactly which ways it closed. Every piece of evidence is filed into one of three classes — and an inferred value may never overwrite a measured one.

measured

A reading taken by a paired instrument, or a value that matches another record in the system. No opinion involved.

28.4 Nm · 14:32:07 · tool #A19  ·  part 45022-KA matches work order
inferred

What a photograph appears to show, judged by a model. Useful, and never permitted to override something that was actually measured.

"pad appears correctly seated" · confidence 0.91
asserted

Craft and judgement — the things no camera can settle. A named human signs for these, and the record says who.

workmanship signed · G. Ruiz · 2026-08-14

We do not judge workmanship from a photograph. No system honestly can. What Warrant establishes is that the work happened, with these parts, on this machine, at this time, to these measured values — and it refuses when it cannot.

The fleet

Nine agents. Each does one narrow job.

None of them calls another. The work one finishes is what wakes the next, and every decision they make is traced end to end.

Scoper

Interviews you until a procedure is unambiguous, then compiles and versions it.

Instructor

Renders each step and answers questions on a held button while hands are busy.

Inspector

Passes a step, asks for more evidence, or escalates to a person. Never fails silently.

Skeptic

Adversarial by design. Does this evidence belong to this job and this machine?

Quartermaster

Parts, stock, and what a shortage is about to block three jobs from now.

Buyer

Drafts purchase orders against shortages. Drafts — never sends.

Registrar

Seals the record and enforces the boundary between measured, inferred and asserted.

Gatekeeper

Holds the machine out of service until the job seals. The one that protects a stranger.

Wright

Meets an unfamiliar instrument, works out how it speaks, and writes the driver itself.

Guardrails

The photographs come from the person being checked.

So the obvious attack is not a forged photo — it is a note in the frame telling the model to approve everything. Every capture is screened before it reaches a verifier.
